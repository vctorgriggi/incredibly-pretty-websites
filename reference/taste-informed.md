# Taste-informed craft

Use for landing pages, portfolios and visual redesigns. Read this once per direction;
do not load an entire pattern library for every implementation or repair.

## Read the brief

Resolve the page's job, audience, existing identity, usable assets and primary action.
State a short design reading, then use the five-block direction contract. Existing
tokens and explicit user choices outrank aesthetic defaults. Distinguish a visual
reference from evidence about the business: a reference never proves services,
testimonials, results or ownership of a photograph.

## Calibrate expression

Choose layout variation, motion and density independently. These are design decisions,
not another configuration file. A local service landing can use expressive photography
with quiet motion and little copy; a task interface needs predictable controls and
visible state. Do not infer luxury, dark mode, elaborate animation or serif typography
from the word “premium”. Preserve an established framework and component system.

## Compose the first viewport

Pick one focal element, a deliberate scale relationship, a useful headline and one
primary action. Let supplied photography or a real product view carry its subject;
do not manufacture client work or replace missing proof with decorative statistics.
Vary alignment, section width, rhythm and media scale according to the story. Repeated
equal cards, a split hero and a color swap do not establish a distinctive composition.

For craft and physical services, show the object or work at a meaningful scale. Use
an open story section to connect verified services to customer needs, then close with
a direct contact action. Do not imitate a dashboard or fill gaps with fake timelines.

## Keep the visual system coherent

Give every color a role and maintain the same accent and radius rules throughout.
Use an intentional bundled typeface, a clear size hierarchy and readable body measure.
Check headline wraps and descender clearance in the actual render. Select type for
the business and available fonts; neither a fashionable family nor a blanket font
ban substitutes for judgment. Preserve brand colors even when they resemble a trend.

Use cards when they express grouping or interaction. Use space and alignment for
ordinary prose. Avoid decorative badges, placeholder reviews, generic illustrations
and busy motion that compete with the action.

## Judge the page as a customer

Read the rendered page from offer to contact. Each section needs a useful job:
explain the service, show relevant work, answer a real question or support the action.
Do not stretch sparse facts into tall narrative blocks. A required section does not
require equal height, a huge heading or another promotional slogan.

Reserve the strongest type scale for the main message. Check the mobile page at
reading size: repeated oversized headings and long blank intervals can make an
otherwise tidy full-page thumbnail exhausting to read. Whitespace earns its place
through grouping and emphasis, not through a claim that it looks intentional.

Judge photographs by what the final crop communicates. For a craft business, the
work should lead, rather than surrounding appliances, walls or doors. Use the
available frame honestly; adjust crop and composition when its subject is weak.
Write in the business's voice for its customer. Source provenance belongs in
evidence, not in captions explaining that a picture came from an official website.

## Make the page work

Keep contact actions real, focus visible, touch controls usable and errors contextual.
Use fluid layouts and CSS Grid where appropriate. Verify mobile navigation, media
crops, button labels, clipping, contrast and empty/loading/error states when present.
Support reduced motion and never hide required content until an animation completes.
Add no framework, motion library or model API solely because a reference uses one.

Inspect the supported rendered platforms, both the overall composition and details
at reading size. Record specific weaknesses before deciding whether the result meets
the brief. A technically responsive page can still have weak hierarchy or generic
composition. Batch concrete corrections and recheck affected states within the task's
repair budget. A visual defect needs a rendered change, not a more favorable report.
Report unobserved evidence and remaining defects honestly.
